# UI highlights

Three people use this app: a patient at a shared tablet who may read only Marathi or Hindi, a desk clerk at peak time, and a doctor between patients. Each decision below names the person it serves. Screenshots: `docs/screens/`. Video of the 60-second judge path: `docs/screens/demo.webm`. Component gallery: `/gallery` (development builds or `DEMO_MODE=1`).

## Patient kiosk
- **Signage, not a website.** One question per screen, set at 36-52 px in the patient's own script, with 28 px tiles at least 96 px tall. Every button on the kiosk is at least 64 px tall, chips included (the whole chip removes the place). Small body regions such as fingers are smaller than that on the figure, so the list alternative is always one tap away. Noto Sans and Noto Sans Devanagari use line heights of 1.3-1.5, so matras and conjuncts are never clipped (checked in en/hi/mr at 800x1280, 1280x800 and 360x740).
- **Selected means a word, not a colour.** A selected tile shows a check icon and "Selected / चुना गया / निवडले". Tiles sink when pressed, giving feedback in 9 ms on the production build.
- **"Not sure" is an answer, Skip is not.** Both sit in their own row under a dashed rule on every question except consent. The server stores them differently, and the doctor sees the difference.
- **The path is adaptive, so progress has no numbers.** A thin bar shows how far along the patient is. "Question 7 of 73" would be untrue.
- **A body map drawn for this product.** Original polygons from `data/body_regions.json`, with a front/back flip and "Patient's right" / "Patient's left" in the patient's language. Chosen places fill teal and appear as removable chips. "Choose from a list" is always one tap away. The server builds and validates the answer.
- **The parchi and the painted floor line.** The token slip looks like the OPD paper slip patients already know: a perforated edge, a 120 px queue number, the doctor and room. It ends in a full-width stripe of the room's floor-line colour and "Follow the green line to Room 12" in the patient's language. Below it, a strip reads "You are here > > > Room 12". Floor-line colours are chosen so any text on them passes AA (table in `docs/DESIGN.md` 3.4).
- **Success only when it is true.** The "You are registered" header and the stamp render only after the server returns the slip. If that fails, the screen says to show it to staff and offers Try again. Tapping Finish twice returns the same number (tested).
- **Built for a shared tablet.** After 90 s without a touch, "Are you still there?" counts down 30 s, then the tablet clears itself. After the slip it clears after 60 s, or straight away with Done. Larger text and high contrast apply to this patient only. Call staff raises a banner on every staff screen. Listen again appears only when the tablet has an on-device voice for the language. Speech recognition is never used.
- **Nothing clinical reaches the patient.** No doses, no rule ids, no other patients. A fired red flag shows only its patient message, on a calm card with a raised-hand icon.

## Staff desk
- **Urgent first, said in words.** New urgent red flags are pinned above the table and pulse once in marigold, then stay still. Each shows "Urgent", the rule's message, "Draft rule, source check pending" and the answers that triggered it. With many urgent patients only the newest three are pinned, so the table stays on screen.
- **Live without reloads.** Server-Sent Events update the board, with a 10 s poll if the stream drops (e2e: a kiosk red flag and a help request appear on an open board with zero reloads). A short chime is generated in the browser, so there is no audio file, and the user can mute it.
- **Fast desk work.** Debounced search and sortable columns (with `aria-sort`). Filters by status and doctor. Select-all covers only the filtered rows, with bulk doctor assignment. The vitals form has server-checked bounds and puts focus on the first error.

## Doctor workstation
- **The dose is the loudest thing, and only when it exists.** For an ok result the dose is set at 44 px in tabular figures. Under it, a ruler draws the rule's range, the delivered dose and the daily total against the daily maximum, all taken from engine fields. For needs input, blocked or refused there is no ruler and no dose number. A unit test feeds non-ok results that still carry dose fields and checks that nothing is shown.
- **One strong action at a time.** "Run dosage check" is primary until a result exists. "Confirm and save to record" turns primary only when the server says the result can be confirmed. On a blocked result, "Record override and show dose" (red) is the only strong action, and it needs a category and a written reason of at least 10 characters. The server enforces both and writes them to the audit log.
- **A tamper-evident log that reads like a chain.** Each row links its hash prefix to the previous entry's prefix. A seal reads "Chain verified" with a count, or "Chain broken", naming the first bad line. There is no edit or delete control anywhere, and a tampered log is shown, never fixed (tested).
- **Calm, dense, two real themes.** Light and dark are designed separately: dark surfaces step up in lightness, and red is lifted to #FF8F85 for contrast. The theme is chosen before first paint. Keyboard: `?` lists the shortcuts, `/` focuses search, `n` opens the next patient, `g` then `q` or `a` navigates, Ctrl+Enter runs the check.

## Promises the app keeps
- **Offline:** fonts and icons ship with the app. An e2e test blocks every request that is not to this computer and records none. The pill reads "Offline: no internet used" and opens to the server's own network check, worded as reported.
- **Accessible:** axe reports zero serious or critical issues on every kiosk screen type, the staff board, the dosage check (blocked and ok), the summary and the audit log, in light and dark. No page scrolls sideways at 360, 800, 1280 or 1440 px.
- **Fast on the LAN:** the kiosk loads 128.7 KB of gzipped JavaScript and first paints in about 0.5 s on the production build.
- **Honest data:** "Prototype data, not clinically verified" appears on every dosing view, and the red "DEMO - UNVERIFIED DATA" bar appears whenever the result is a demo. All patients are synthetic. All Hindi and Marathi text is machine-drafted and listed for native review by `scripts/list_unreviewed_translations.py`.
